BLOCK B - E-VOA 30 DAYS DESCRIPTION
PAGE: /product/e-voa-30-days/
------------------------------------------------------------
E-VOA (electronic Visa on Arrival) is a 30-day Indonesian tourist visa bought online before you fly to Bali, so you skip the airport VOA queue and go straight to the immigration counter on arrival. Visa4Bali processes E-VOA applications in 1 to 2 working days for Rp750,000, a fee that already includes both the e-VOA and the government e-VOA charge. The visa grants an initial 30-day stay and can be extended once for a further 30 days, giving a maximum total stay of 60 days. To apply you need three things: a passport with at least 6 months' validity remaining, a clear photo of the passport holder, and your onward flight ticket. Because approval happens before departure, E-VOA is the fastest way to secure a 30-day Bali tourist visa without queuing.

BLOCK C - TOURIST VISA 211 EXTENSION DESCRIPTION
PAGE: /product/social-visa-b-211-ru/
------------------------------------------------------------
The Tourist Visa 211/C1 lets you stay in Indonesia for up to 180 days without leaving the country, and Visa4Bali handles the full extension process for Rp1,800,000.

The visa gives an initial 60-day stay, which can then be extended twice, 60 days at a time, to reach the 180-day maximum. Each extension is issued as an electronic stay permit with a soft-copy file and a barcode registered in the immigration system, so there is no physical sticker to collect. Weekends and Indonesian public holidays are not counted as processing days, so apply before your current permit expires; the extension can still be completed shortly after. To start, Visa4Bali needs only your passport, and the team prepares every required application document and arranges a local sponsor on your behalf.

BLOCK D - REMOTE WORKING VISA (E33G) DESCRIPTION
PAGE: /product/remote-working-visa-e33g/
------------------------------------------------------------
The Remote Working Visa (E33G) is a 12-month, multiple-entry Indonesian stay permit for foreigners employed by a company based outside Indonesia, letting them live in Bali, and bring their family, while working remotely. Visa4Bali arranges the E33G for Rp12,000,000 to Rp13,500,000, which includes the US$150 government tax, sponsorship, the e-visa, the government e-visa fee, and a 100% approval guarantee. Processing takes 5 to 6 working days. To qualify you must show income of at least US$60,000 per year, proven with either a salary statement from your employer or bank-account records. The documents required are a passport valid for at least 6 months, a clear photo of the passport holder, and the income proof described above. Because it is multiple-entry and valid for a full year, the E33G suits digital nomads and remote employees who want a longer, more stable base in Bali than a tourist visa allows.

Q1. How do I apply for a 211 tourist visa with Visa4Bali, and what documents do I need?

To apply for a 211/C1 tourist visa with Visa4Bali, first contact us by WhatsApp or email to confirm the visa details, or book directly through this website. To begin processing we need only two things from you: a clear photo of your passport (make sure it has at least 6 months of validity remaining) and a photo of the passport holder. Once we have these, our team prepares the full application and liaises directly with the Indonesian immigration office on your behalf. You do not need to visit an immigration office yourself; we handle document preparation, submission, and follow-up from start to finish.


Q2. How long does a Bali tourist visa take to process?

Tourist visa processing with Visa4Bali takes 5 to 6 working days for the standard service, or 3 working days if you choose the expedited option; pick whichever fits your travel plans. Working days exclude Saturdays, Sundays, and Indonesian public holidays, so allow for those when timing your application. We recommend starting the process before your current stay permit expires, so your new visa or extension is ready in time and you avoid any risk of an overstay fine.


Q3. How do I extend my visa in Bali?

To extend your visa in Indonesia you need the required application documents and a sponsor based in Indonesia. If you would like Visa4Bali to handle the extension, we need only your passport, and our team prepares every application document and provides the local sponsor for you. The Tourist Visa 211/C1 can be extended twice, 60 days at a time, for a maximum stay of 180 days. Each extension is issued as an electronic stay permit with a barcode registered in the immigration system. Apply before your current permit expires; weekends and public holidays are not counted as processing days.


Q4. What happens if I overstay my visa in Indonesia?

Under Indonesian immigration regulations (Law No. 6 of 2011 on Immigration), overstaying a valid visa or stay permit incurs a fine of IDR 1,000,000 per day. If you have overstayed by more than 60 days, you must pay the accumulated fine and will be deported from Indonesia, and a re-entry ban may also apply. If you are already in an overstay situation, contact Visa4Bali as soon as possible; our team can advise on paying the fine correctly and arranging your departure, or, where still possible, a stay-permit resolution to minimise penalties.
